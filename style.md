## Style rules (hard)

1. No puffery about significance, legacy, or broader trends.
   Banned phrases include: stands as, serves as, is a testament to, is a reminder of, plays a vital/significant/crucial/pivotal/key role, plays a key moment, underscores its importance, highlights its significance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting, contributing to the, setting the stage for, marking/shaping the, represents a shift, marks a shift, key turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted.
   Do not inflate the importance of any subject.

2. No superficial trailing analysis. In particular, no present-participle ("-ing") clause tacked onto a sentence to add significance or context.
   Bad: "It holds a pivotal place in X, serving as a major hub with historical significance."

3. Neutral tone. Banned: boasts a, vibrant, rich, profound, enhancing, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, featuring, diverse array.

4. No weasel wording. Banned patterns: "industry reports", "observers have cited", "experts argue", "some critics argue", "several sources" when only a few are cited, "such as" introducing exhaustive lists.

5. No outline-style "challenges and future prospects" conclusions. Banned: "Despite its X, Y faces several challenges", "Despite these challenges", "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook" as a closer.

6. No AI vocabulary. Banned: additionally (especially sentence-initial), align with, boasts (meaning "has"), bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (as a verb), interplay, intricate, intricacies, key (as an adjective), landscape (as an abstract noun), meticulous, meticulously, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry (as an abstract noun), testament, underscore (as a verb), valuable, vibrant.
   Especially avoid: emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing, and anything that overclaims notability, attribution, or media coverage.

7. Prefer basic copulatives ("is", "are") over "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents [a]", "boasts", "features", "maintains", "offers [a]", "refers to".

8. Never use these AI tells:
   - "Not just X, but also Y"
   - "Not X, but Y"

9. Avoid the rule of three. No "adjective, adjective, adjective"; no "short phrase, short phrase, and short phrase".

10. Never use boldface.

11. Never use em dashes.

12. Straight quotation marks only ("..." or '...'). No curly quotes.
